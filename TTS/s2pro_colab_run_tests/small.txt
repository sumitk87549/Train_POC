"Yaar," Sherlock Holmes bola jo hum fireplace ke dono taraf baithe hue hain Baker Street pe uske ghar mein, "life bohot hi strange hai, tere dimaag se bhi zyada. Hum kabhi sochte bhi nahi ki duniya mein kya-kya hota rehta hai. Agar hum idhar se ud sakein, is city ke upar mandraayein, roofs hata dein aur dekhein andar kya chal raha hai – kitni ajeeb baatein, coincidence, planning, cross-purposes, events jo peedhi dar peedhi chalti aa rahi hain… yeh sab fiction ko bhi bore kar dega."

"Phir bhi mujhe toh convince nahi kiya," maine jawab diya. "Jo cases paper mein aate hain woh mostly simple aur vulgar hote hain. Police reports mein realism itna zyada hota hai ki, honestly, neither fascinating lagta hai na artistic."

Holmes ne kaha, “Realistic effect banane ke liye thoda select karna padhta hai, discretion use karna padhta hai.”  “Yeh police report mein missing hai, jisme magistrate ki baatein pe zyada focus kiya jata hai details par nahi. Details hi toh poori baat ka essence hote hain. Yaad rakhna, common cheezein bohot unnatural hoti hain."

Maine muskuraya aur head shake kar diya. "I can totally understand what you're saying," maine bola. “Obviously, tere position mein – unofficial advisor ban ke har kisi ki help karna jo absolutely puzzled hai, three continents mein – tu sab strange aur bizarre dekhta hi rahega. Lekin yahan” - Maine morning paper uthaya ground se -  “chalo practical test karte hain. Yeh pehla headline hai. 'A husband's cruelty to his wife.' Half a column print hai, lekin mujhe bina padhe bhi pata hai yeh bohot familiar story hogi. Dusri aurat, drink, push, blow, bruise… sympathetic sister ya landlady.  Koi bhi writer isse zyada crude nahi likh sakta.”

“Actually,” Holmes ne kaha paper leke usmein jhalak dekh kar, “tera example toh tere argument ke against hi ja raha hai." "Yeh Dundas separation case hai, aur incidentally, main isme kuch small points clear karne mein busy tha. Husband teetotaler tha, koi dusri aurat nahi thi, aur complaint yeh thi ki woh har meal ke end pe apne false teeth nikal kar apni wife par fek deta tha.  Honestly, yeh idea kisi story-teller ko bhi nahi aa sakta.”

Holmes ne apna gold snuffbox nikala, uske upar ek bada amethyst laga hua tha. “Snuff lele Doctor,” bolkar maine taraf badhaya, "aur maano ki is example mein main jeet gaya." Uska splendour itna contrast kar raha tha uske simple life se ki mujhe comment karne se rok nahi paya.

“Ah," Holmes ne kaha, “main bhool gaya tha ki tumhein kuch hafton se dekha nahi hai.”  "Yeh King of Bohemia ka ek souvenir hai, meri help ke badle mein Irene Adler papers waale case mein."

“Aur ring?” maine poocha uski finger pe chamak rahi brilliant dekh kar. "Woh Holland ke reigning family ne diya tha, lekin woh matter itna delicate tha ki main tujhe bhi nahi bata sakta.”

"Aur kya tere paas koi cases chal rahe hain?" Maine interest se poochha. “Around ten or twelve, but none of them are interesting right now. Important hai, par interesting nahi. Actually, mujhe lagta hai ke unimportant matters mein hi observation aur cause and effect ka analysis karne ki scope hoti hai – wohi toh investigation ko charm deta hai.”

“Bade crimes mostly simple hote hain,” Holmes ne continue kiya. “Because crime jitna bada hota hai, motive utna obvious hota jata hai. Isme se ek case Marseilles se aaya hai jo thoda complex hai, lekin baaki sab boring hain. Par shayad kuch der mein mujhe koi interesting mil jaaye…kyunki yeh mere clients mein se ek aa raha hai.”

Woh khadi ho gaya aur window ke beech mein khada hokar London ki street dekhne laga. Maine uske upar se dekha toh opposite pavement par ek badi aurat khadi thi, uska neck heavy fur boa se ghira hua tha, aur broad-brimmed hat pe red feather Duchess of Devonshire style mein tilted kiya gaya tha. Woh apni window ke taraf nervous ho kar peek rahi thi, uska body back and forth oscillate kar raha tha, aur uski ungliyan glove buttons se khel rahi thin.
